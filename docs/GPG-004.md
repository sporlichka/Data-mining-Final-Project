# GPG-004 — Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

- **Status:** in review
- **Owner:** Tastemir (lead), Stanislav (statistical tests)
- **Blocked by:** [GPG-003](GPG-003.md)
- **Parallel with:** —
- **Feeds into:** [GPG-005](GPG-005.md)

## Scope

- Summary statistics
- Feature distributions
- Correlation analysis
- At least 5 meaningful visualizations
- At least 5 important conclusions from the analysis
- ANOVA or another statistical test where appropriate (e.g. wage by sex, by
  occupation, by education level)

## Proposed Solution

**Implementation:** [`notebooks/GPG-004_eda.ipynb`](../notebooks/GPG-004_eda.ipynb) — run
[`GPG-003_preprocessing.ipynb`](../notebooks/GPG-003_preprocessing.ipynb) first (it creates `data/processed/cps_clean.csv.gz`).
The notebook writes its numbers to [`results/GPG-004_findings.md`](results/GPG-004_findings.md) (conclusions with the actual values,
test tables, regression summary) and saves figures to `docs/figures/GPG-004/`. Conclusions are generated from the data, so the
wording follows the results of the real run.

Target: `ln_realhrwage` (log real hourly wage). Gap statistics are **weighted** with the CPS weight `wtsupp`; sample-composition
charts and hypothesis tests are unweighted. With ~340k rows every test is "significant", so each is paired with an **effect size**.

### Contents

| Scope item | Where in the notebook | What is done |
|---|---|---|
| Summary statistics | §2 | `describe` + skewness/kurtosis; weighted mean/median by sex; frequency tables (education, race, occupation) incl. women's share |
| Feature distributions | §3, Fig. 1–3 | wage vs log-wage by sex; age / experience / hours / weeks; education & occupation composition by sex |
| Correlation analysis | §4, Fig. 4–5 | Pearson and Spearman with the target; correlation matrix of numeric features; top-15 binary features |
| Visualizations (≥ 5) | Fig. 1–10 | 1 wage distribution · 2 numeric distributions · 3 composition · 4 correlation matrix · 5 top binary correlations · 6 gap over time · 7 wage by education × sex · 8 experience profile and gap · 9 occupational segregation · 10 gender coefficient as controls are added |
| Statistical tests | §7 | Welch t-test, Mann–Whitney U, Cohen's d (wage by sex); one-way ANOVA + η² + Kruskal–Wallis for education, occupation, region, race, marital status, class of worker, year; Levene; Tukey HSD (education); two-way ANOVA sex × education; χ² + Cramér's V (sex × occupation) |
| Conclusions (≥ 5) | §9 | 10 data-driven conclusions (listed in `results/GPG-004_findings.md`) |

### Preview of the research questions

| RQ | What the EDA shows | Where |
|---|---|---|
| RQ1 raw gap and trend | weighted hourly gap per survey year (geometric-mean and median), pooled gap, linear trend (pp/year, p-value), annual-income gap for comparison with GPG-002 | §5, Fig. 6, `results/gap_by_year.csv` |
| RQ2 explained vs unexplained | stepwise Mincer OLS: `female` coefficient after adding year → education/experience → hours → occupation/region → race/marital status; explained share of the raw log gap | §8, Fig. 10, `results/regression_models.csv` |
| RQ3 sex still significant | robust (HC1) CI and p-value of the `female` coefficient in the full specification | §8 |
| RQ4 feature importance, men vs women | R² gain per block; the same model fitted separately for men and women (returns to education, hours, experience) | §8.1 |

### Notes for the team
